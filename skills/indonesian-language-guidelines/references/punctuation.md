## 4. Punctuation

### 4.1. How to use punctuation marks

The following is the rules of using the punctuation marks in Indonesian.

- **Period | .**
  - Used at the end of a declarative sentence (e.g. *Mereka duduk di sana.*).
  - Used after a number or letter in a chart, overview, or a list, excluding a number or letter which is already followed by a bracket.
  - Used to separate hours, minutes and seconds, which indicate time or timeframe (e.g. *pukul 01.35.20*).
  - Used to indicate group of thousands (e.g. *Indonesia memiliki lebih dari 17.000 pulau.*).
  - Used after an abbreviation which consists of three letters or more (e.g. *dst.* for *dan seterusnya*).
  - Used after each of the abbreviation elements of a person name, degree, term of address, rank title, or position title (e.g. *W. Bush*).

- **Comma | ,**
  - Used after each element in an enumeration (e.g. *Buku, majalah, dan jurnal termasuk sumber kepustakaan.*).
  - Used before a conjunction in a compound sentence (e.g. *Ini bukan milik saya, melainkan milik ayah saya.*).
  - Used to separate the subordinate clauses (e.g. *Kalau diundang, saya akan datang.*).
  - Used after an inter-clause conjunction (e.g. *Pendaftaran akan ditutup dalam dua hari. Oleh karena itu, kami mohon agar Anda segera mendaftar.*).
  - Used to separate a direct quotation (declarative quotation) from other parts of the sentence (e.g. *"Kita harus berbagi dalam hidup ini," kata nenek saya.*).
  - Used between a person's name and the abbreviation of his/her academic degree (e.g. *Ratulangi, S.E.*).
  - Used to enclose additional information (e.g. *Soekarno, Presiden I RI, merupakan salah seorang pendiri Gerakan Nonblok.*).
  - Used after a prepositional phrase at the beginning of a sentence (e.g. *Atas perhatian Saudara, kami ucapkan terima kasih.*).

- **Colon | :**
  - Used to introduce an enumeration or explanation (e.g. *Mereka memerlukan perabot rumah tangga: kursi, meja, dan lemari.*).
  - Used after a word or phrase which requires description (e.g. *Ketua : Ahmad Wijaya*).

- **Semicolon | ;**
  - Used as a substitute for a conjunction in a compound sentence (e.g. *Hari sudah malam; anak-anak masih membaca buku.*).
  - Used at the end of each clause in numbered lists.

  Example:
  - *Agenda rapat ini meliputi:*
    - *pemilihan ketua, sekretaris, dan bendahara;*
    - *penyusunan anggaran dasar, anggaran rumah tangga, dan program kerja; dan*
    - *pendataan anggota, dokumentasi, dan aset organisasi.*

- **Exclamation point | !**
  - Used at the end of an expression or statement which is in the form of exclamation or command (e.g. *Bayarlah pajak tepat pada waktunya! Alangkah indahnya taman laut di Bunaken!*).

- **Question mark | ?**
  - Used at the end of interrogative sentences (e.g. *Siapa pencipta lagu "Indonesia Raya"?*).
  - Used to mark parts of sentences that are doubtful or questionable (e.g. *Di Indonesia terdapat 740 (?) bahasa daerah.*).

- **Hyphen | -**
  - Used to connect reduplication (e.g. *anak-anak; berulang-ulang*).
  - Used to connect day, month and year when written in numbers (e.g. *11-11-2013*).
  - Used to connect an Indonesian element with local language or foreign language elements in a word (e.g. *di-sowan-i; me-recall*).
  - Used to connect:
    1. *se-* with the following word when initiated by a capital letter (e.g. *Se-Indonesia*);
    2. *ke-* with a number (e.g. *Peringkat ke-2*);
    3. a number with *-an* (e.g. *Tahun 1950-an*);
    4. a prefix or a word with the abbreviation using capital letters (e.g. *Hari-H*);
    5. a word with God's possessive pronoun (e.g. *Ciptaan-Nya*);
    6. a letter with a number (e.g. *D-3, S-1*).

- **Dash | —**

  In Indonesian, there is only one type of dash, which is two times a hyphen.
  - Used to mark the insertion of words or sentences that give additional information (e.g. *Keberhasilan itu—kita sependapat—dapat dicapai jika kita mau berusaha keras.*).
  - Used to assert the apposition (e.g. *Gerakan Pengutamaan Bahasa Indonesia—amanat Sumpah Pemuda—harus terus digelorakan.*).
  - Used between numbers, dates or places to represent the meaning of 'to' (e.g. *Tahun 2010—2013; Tanggal 5—10 April 2013; Jakarta—Bandung*).

- **Slash | /**
  - Used in the letter number, e.g. *7/PK/II/2022*
  - Used in address number, e.g. *Jalan Kramat III/10*
  - Used in indicating a 1 year period that is divided into 2 calendar years, e.g. *tahun ajaran 2023/2024*
  - Used as a substitute for the word 'and', e.g. *AD/ART = anggaran dasar dan anggaran rumah tangga*
  - Used as a substitute for the word 'or', e.g. *orang tua/wali = orang tua atau wali*
  - Used as a substitute for the word 'every', e.g. *150 km/jam = 150 kilometer setiap jam*
  - Used to enclose letters, words, or groups of words as a correction or reduction of errors or excesses in the original text written by others, e.g. */h/utang* means correction of the original writing "hutang" to "utang"
  - Used as a date element separator, e.g. *10/1/2024* (January 10, 2024)
  - Used as a division operation marker. Arithmetic operations are usually given a space before and after, e.g. *10 ∕ 2 = 5*
  - Used when writing multi-line texts — such as rhymes, poems, or song lyrics — into a single line. Slashes are used to mark line changes; to separate a verse break, two slashes are sometimes used; there are spaces before and after the slashes.

    Example, from Chairil Anwar's poem: *Kalau sampai waktu / 'ku mau tak seorang 'kan merayu / Tidak juga kau // Tak perlu sedu sedan itu*

- **Double quotation marks | " "**
  - Used to enclose a direct quotation (e.g. *"Merdeka atau mati!" seru Bung Tomo dalam pidatonya.*).
  - Used to enclose the title of a song, poem, film, drama, article, etc., when it's used within a sentence (e.g. *Film "Ainun dan Habibie" merupakan kisah nyata yang diangkat dari sebuah novel.*).
  - Used to enclose a less popular term or a special word (e.g. *"Tetikus" komputer ini sudah tidak berfungsi.*).

- **Single quotation marks | ' '**
  - Used to enclose a quotation within a quotation (e.g. *Tanya dia, "Kaudengar bunyi 'kring-kring' tadi?"*).
  - Used to enclose a definition, translation, or explanation of a word or expression (e.g. *wisdom 'kebijaksanaan'; noken 'tas khas Papua'*).

- **Brackets | ( )**
  - Used to enclose additional information or explanation (e.g. *Sajak Tranggono yang berjudul "Ubud" (nama tempat yang terkenal di Bali) ditulis pada tahun 1962.*).
  - Used to enclose a letter or number used for an enumeration listing (e.g. *Faktor produksi menyangkut (a) bahan baku, (b) biaya produksi, dan (c) tenaga kerja.*).

### 4.2. Punctuation in greetings and closings

In Indonesian, both greetings and closings should be followed by a comma. After the greeting, the next sentence should be initiated with a capital letter.

Examples:
- Source text: *With respect, / We thank you for your input.*
  - ✘ *Dengan hormat / kami mengucapkan terima kasih atas masukan Anda.*
  - ✓ *Dengan hormat, / Kami mengucapkan terima kasih atas masukan Anda.*

- Source text: *Best regards, / Soekarno*
  - ✘ *Hormat saya / Soekarno*
  - ✓ *Hormat saya, / Soekarno*
